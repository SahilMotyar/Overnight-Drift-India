# PLAN.md

**Question.** On Indian market data, does the price path split the way the reel
says: up overnight (close -> next open), flat or down intraday (open -> close)?
And can any part of that split be traded after Indian costs?

**Not the question.** Whether the decomposition identity holds. It always does.
Night x day = buy-and-hold is arithmetic, not a finding. What matters is the
*sign and size* of each half, and whether it survives costs.

**Deliverable.** `reports/findings.md` with:
- night/day/hold curves for Nifty 50, Sensex, Bank Nifty, a tradable Nifty ETF
  and the Nifty 50 stocks
- significance tests with honest error bars
- a break-even cost table showing, per instrument, how many bp per round trip
  the overnight edge can pay for.

---

## Background

- The reel describes **Bruce Knuteson** (the reel spells it "Knutson"), a former
  MIT physicist who worked at D. E. Shaw. Main paper: *Strikingly Suspicious
  Overnight and Intraday Returns*, arXiv:2010.01727 (Oct 2020). It covers many
  national indices, India included.
- His explanation (the footprint of one large participant's trading) is
  contested. Other work on the same effect:
  - Cliff, Cooper & Gulen (2008), *Return differences between trading and
    non-trading hours: like night and day*.
  - Lou, Polk & Skouras (2019, JFE), *A tug of war: overnight versus intraday
    expected returns*. Explains it with investor clienteles.

## Definitions

For trading day `t`, using adjusted prices:

```
night_t = Open_t  / Close_{t-1} - 1     # close -> next open (spans weekends/holidays)
day_t   = Close_t / Open_t      - 1     # open -> close
(1 + night_t) * (1 + day_t) = Close_t / Close_{t-1}     # exact, every day
```

Chained: `N = prod(1 + night)`, `D = prod(1 + day)`, `N * D = buy-and-hold`.

Stats and plots use log returns: `log N + log D = log hold`. In log space the two
halves add and are symmetric, so the "+138M% vs -99.9%" lopsidedness the reel
explains (a loss stops at zero) goes away.

### Phase 2: Validation
`python -m scripts.phase2_validate`. It reads the Phase 1 cache only, asks NSE
about disputed days (cached), and writes `data/clean/<ticker>.csv` plus
`reports/phase2_*.csv`. From 317,503 raw rows it keeps 303,657, with
**300,282 usable nights** and 303,013 usable days (after the dividend fix in
item 10, found at the Phase 3 checkpoint).

The check that mattered most was **asking NSE**. NSE publishes a bhavcopy
for every session and only for sessions, and its archive goes back to Nov
1994. So a disputed day is settled by whether that file exists. Its prices
also give an independent check of Yahoo's.

1. **Calendar** (7,651 sessions, 1996-01-01 .. 2026-10-06).
   - *Candidates.* A date is a candidate if at least 50% of the stocks listed
     at the time (min. 5) have a real row on it.
   - *Disputes.* 142 dates were disputed (stocks vs the index calendar, or the
     stocks split). NSE settled them: 132 sessions, 10 not, e.g. Sensex rows
     on 2000-01-26, which is Republic Day.
   - *Hidden sessions.* Every day the calendar treated as closed (2,292 dates)
     was also put to NSE. **NSE traded on 49 of them, and 48 appear in no Yahoo
     series at all**:
     - 16 Muhurat sessions
     - 28 Saturdays: special sessions in 1997-2014, Budget days (1999-02-27,
       2015-02-28, 2020-02-01) and the 2024 DR-drill Saturdays
     - 5 other days (2009-01-01, 2012-10-26, 2012-11-11, 2019-02-13,
       2019-03-29)

     Without this, the night across each one silently contains a whole
     trading day.
   - *Special sessions.* 30 Muhurat sessions (one per Diwali, 1996-2025) and
     30 weekend sessions.
2. **Rows removed** (ticker x reason in `reports/phase2_drop_table.csv`):
   - before the series' start date: 6,667
   - placeholders (flat at previous close, zero volume; Yahoo's holiday
     filler): 5,124 across 51 series
   - before NSE's equity segment opened (TMPV, 1991-94): 1,001
   - Open/Close outside [Low, High] (all in 1996): 462
   - flat zero-volume bars: 293
   - not an NSE session: 289
   - scale glitches, e.g. TATASTEEL +950% then -90%: 9
   - price <= 0: 1
3. **Nights rejected:**
   - spans a session the series lacks: 2,063
   - special session at either end: 1,203
   - first row: 54
   - stale index open: 43 (indices only)
   - beyond +-20%: 19

   Days rejected: special session 602, stale index open 43.
4. **Stale opens.** Indices: an error, so rejected. Stocks: **real**. NSE's
   official OPEN equals PREVCLOSE on these days (23/23 sampled), and they make
   up 4.0% of all usable stock nights, so they stay in. Dropping them would
   bias the night upward.
5. **Reversed opens** (night and day both beyond 15%, opposite directions,
   nearly cancelling). These were first rejected as "bad opens". NSE shows the
   same open on 60 of the 61 it could check (e.g. NIFTYBEES opened -19.5% on
   2016-11-29 and recovered +24.5% the same day). They are freak opening
   prints, not data errors, so they are **flagged** (`reversed_open`, 98
   rows), not rejected.
6. **Nights beyond +-20%** (`reports/phase2_unusual_vs_nse.csv`). All 19 are
   rejected, because a corporate action can't be told apart from a real move
   without a corporate-action database. Against NSE:
   - Yahoo artifacts (3): ADANIENT 2015-06-03 (+220%), RELIANCE 1997-10-27,
     TRENT 2026-01-01.
   - Corporate actions, with NSE's PREVCLOSE unadjusted too (2): RELIANCE
     1997-11-05 (bonus), TMPV 2025-10-14 (demerger).
   - **Genuine price moves (7):** EICHERMOT x5 (1998-2001), ONGC 2002-05-06,
     NESTLEIND 2010-04-23. See open decisions.
   - Not checkable (7).
7. **Start dates** (`reports/phase2_series.csv`). A series starts after its
   last 250-session window with under 90% coverage. Coverage is counted only
   against sessions at least one Yahoo series has; otherwise the hidden
   sessions drag every start past them. Results:
   - NESTLEIND starts 2010-02-08, after 7 years of placeholders.
   - NIFTYBEES starts 2013-06-24, after the 2012-13 no-trade spell.
   - Most 1996 starts move to mid-1996.
   - Four "not before" overrides, each backed by a jump in the data: TCS
     2004-08-25 (IPO), ULTRACEMCO 2004-08-24, BAJAJ-AUTO and BAJAJFINSV
     2008-05-26 (demerger). Three of them bind; BAJAJ-AUTO's coverage date
     (2008-11-21) is later anyway.
   - Two starts moved by implausible dividends (item 10): ADANIENT
     2004-06-28, EICHERMOT 1997-07-21.
8. **Night labels.** Every row gets a `night_kind` (weeknight / weekend /
   holiday / gap / first) and `night_days`, its calendar length.
9. **Spot-check vs NSE** (`reports/phase2_spotcheck.csv`): 40 random sessions,
   1998-2026.
   - *Accepted returns match NSE to within 0.2%:* stocks 1,309 nights / 1,351
     days, indices 36 / 38 (from 2012; the archive has no earlier files), ETF
     15 / 15, all 100%. 152 stock lookups failed because of old symbols or
     ISINs.
   - *Close.* **Yahoo's Close is NSE's official close** (the 30-minute VWAP),
     not the last trade, on all 554 days where those two differ.
   - *The first draw*, made before the hidden-session fix, matched 98.0%.
     Its 27 mismatches: 20 nights across hidden Saturdays (since fixed), 2
     where NSE's own PREVCLOSE ignores a bonus (KOTAKBANK 2015-07-08, NTPC
     2019-03-19), and 5 genuine Yahoo errors (wrong closes on 2019-03-18,
     BAJAJFINSV 2012-09-06). So expect roughly **0.4% of nights carrying
     undetected Yahoo errors**.
10. **Implausible dividends** (added at the Phase 3 checkpoint). Phase 2
    checked raw prices, but Phase 3 uses dividend-adjusted ones, and those had
    gone negative for ADANIENT's first 46 rows. The cause: 4 Yahoo dividends
    were never scaled for later splits, at 41-121% of the previous close
    (ADANIENT 2002-09-04, 2003-09-04, 2004-06-28; EICHERMOT 1997-07-21).
    Yahoo's factor `1 - D / prev Close` then mis-scales every earlier price,
    and the ex-date nights came out at -570%, +767%, +108% and +66%.
    - The rule: a dividend above 20% of the previous close starts the series
      on that ex-date. The largest real one is Coal India's 9.6% (2014-01-17),
      so the gap between real and mis-scaled dividends is wide.
    - `finish` now refuses to write any adjusted price <= 0.
- TMPV: only the demerger night is rejected, so the history is kept from
  1996-06-05. This replaces "start after the demerger", which would have kept
  one year.
- CHECKPOINT: done. Drop table, start dates, spot-check, reviewed rejections.

### Phase 3: Decomposition
`python -m scripts.phase3_decompose`. It reads `data/clean/` only and writes
`reports/phase3_*.csv` and `reports/figures/phase3_*.png`.
- *Inputs.* A night counts only where `night_ok`, a day only where `day_ok`.
  Real zero nights (official Open = previous close, 4.0% of stock nights)
  stay in.
- *Identity.* `(1 + night)(1 + day) = Close / prev Close` holds to 2.2e-16
  on every series. The Open cancels, so this guards the implementation (both
  legs from the same rows), not the data.
- *Two holds.* `split_hold` = nights x days exactly. Buy-and-hold also
  contains the returns that can't be split (mostly nights across sessions
  Yahoo lacks), so it runs a little higher (Nifty x5.04 vs x4.16).

**Results.** Growth of Rs 1 by leg; mean / median bp per return:

| Series | Nights | Days | Night mean / median | Day mean / median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nifty 50 (2007-) | x55.5 (+23.5%/yr) | x0.075 (-12.7%/yr) | +8.9 / +7.7 | -4.9 / -4.6 |
| Sensex (1997-) | x14,543 (+38.8%/yr) | /1,203 (-21.5%/yr) | +13.8 / +15.1 | -9.1 / -6.3 |
| Nifty Bank (2007-) | x67.6 (+24.8%/yr) | x0.11 (-11.1%/yr) | +9.5 / +12.3 | -3.7 / -3.6 |
| NIFTYBEES (2013-) | x359M | /106M | +63.4 / +22.7 | -54.1 / -25.7 |

- **The reel's pattern holds everywhere.** All 50 stocks: nights beat
  days. 49 of 50 lose money in the day; the exception is INDIGO, at +2.9%/yr.
  Across stocks, the median of the mean night is +17.3 bp and of the mean day
  -6.9 bp. 61% of stock nights are up against 46% of days.
- **NIFTYBEES's night/day split comes from its opening price, not the
  market.** Until 2018 its nights track ^NSEI within about +-12 bp a year.
  From 2019 the ETF opens far above where the index opens: the median gap
  is +186 bp a night in 2021 and still +23-26 bp in 2024-26, and the day
  gives it back. Overnight, the ETF and the index can't really move apart
  by this much, so the ETF's official open isn't a price the index supports.
  This matters for Phase 5/6, where the ETF was to be the realistic way to
  trade the index.
- **Stocks vs the index.** From 2011 the equal-weight stock night tracks
  ^NSEI within about 0-7 bp a year. In 2007-2010 it runs 11-38 bp above.
  That fits the late-2010 pre-open call auction (before it, a stock's "open"
  was its first trade), and it is why Phase 4 keeps pre-2011 as its own
  subperiod.
- **Nights by length.** Weekend nights do not earn more than weeknights:
  Nifty earns 5.1 bp per weekend night vs 9.3 bp per weeknight, and all
  stocks pooled earn 16.2 vs 18.4 bp. Per calendar day, a weekend earns about
  a third as much. If the drift were pay for bearing risk while the market
  is shut, longer nights should earn more. Holiday nights earn the most
  (Nifty 16.5 bp; n = 239).
- **Robustness.**
  - Dropping reversed opens moves the median stock night from 17.3 to 17.2 bp.
  - Dropping ex-dividend nights moves it to 17.0 bp, so the night premium is
    not dividends.
  - Restoring the 7 genuine +-20% nights moves EICHERMOT from 19.8 to
    21.3 bp, ONGC from 21.6 to 21.8 bp and NESTLEIND from 12.2 to 11.7 bp.
- **Charts:**
  - `phase3_nifty.png` (the reel's chart, on Nifty)
  - `phase3_indices.png`
  - `phase3_stocks.png` (all 50)
  - `phase3_longest_stock.png` (Tata Steel 1996-2026: nights x59.6M, days
    /2.8M, the Micron analogue)

  They follow the dataviz method: nights blue, days orange (palette
  validated), "nights x days" gray, and a log axis.
- CHECKPOINT: done. Summary table, Nifty chart, night kinds, sensitivity,
  vs-index gaps.

### Phase 4: Is it real?
Subperiods are fixed now, before any results are seen:
- 2007-2010: before the pre-open auction
- 2011-2019
- 2020 to Jun 2023: COVID and the retail surge
- Jul 2023 onward: SGX Nifty moved to GIFT City, so the Indian night is now
  priced live offshore

`python -m scripts.phase4_significance` (about 1 min). It writes
`reports/phase4_tests.csv` (every series x period) and
`reports/phase4_cross_section.csv`.

Method:
- *Means.* Night and day means get Newey-West t-stats (numpy, 10 lags
  for ~7,600 sessions).
- *Gap.* The gap is night - day on the same date, with both accepted. It
  gets its own t-stat and a 95% stationary-bootstrap CI (2,000 reps, mean
  block length about 20 sessions).
- *Aggregate.* The stocks' headline is one test: an equal-weight portfolio
  (each date, the mean over the stocks with that return).

**Gap = night - day, bp per session (t; 95% CI):**

| Period | Nifty 50 | Sensex | Nifty Bank | EW stocks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| full | **13.6** (7.2; 9.3-17.6) | **23.0** (13.1) | **13.1** (5.9) | **28.4** (16.1; 24.4-32.6) |
| pre-2011 | 0.2 (0.0; -15.7-16.8) | **24.3** (7.7) | 5.4 (0.7) | **38.7** (13.0) |
| 2011-2019 | **17.7** (8.7) | **26.3** (13.3) | **17.3** (6.0) | **18.4** (8.9) |
| 2020-Jun 2023 | **20.6** (4.8) | **21.8** (5.1) | **20.2** (3.3) | **23.3** (5.6) |
| Jul 2023- | **8.1** (3.2; 3.3-13.0) | **9.5** (3.4) | 1.6 (0.5) | **14.1** (5.1) |

- **The split is real.** Nifty's night averages +8.9 bp (t = 9.6) and its
  day -4.9 bp (t = -3.0). The night is the stronger half: the day's negative
  mean is significant in the full sample but not in every period.
- **Across stocks** (`reports/phase4_tests.csv`):
  - Full sample: the gap clears t > 2 for 47 of 50 stocks, and the
    Bonferroni bar for all 250 stock tests (|t| > 3.72) for 40.
  - No stock has a significantly negative gap or night in any period.
  - Nights clear t > 2 for 49 of 50, while days are significantly negative
    for only 27 of 50.
- **The gap is shrinking.** Since Jul 2023 (GIFT Nifty), Nifty's gap is
  8.1 bp, against 17-21 bp before. Nifty Bank's is no longer significant,
  and only 22 of 50 stocks clear t > 2 (12 at Bonferroni). Nights are now
  priced live offshore in GIFT City; that fits, but doesn't prove, the
  timing.
- **Pre-2011 Nifty shows no gap** (0.2 bp, t = 0.0, 2007-2010 only, with the
  2008 crash). Sensex over 1997-2010 shows a strong one. The pre-auction
  window is short and noisy for Nifty, and the stocks' early opens were first
  trades (Phase 3). Don't read much into either.
- **No link to liquidity.** From 2011, over 50 stocks, Spearman rho between
  traded value and the gap is -0.16 (permutation p = 0.26). By liquidity
  tercile, the median gap is 19.2 / 16.8 / 17.8 bp.
- **Not tested:** size (there is no market-cap history) and F&O inclusion
  (every current constituent is in F&O).
- **NIFTYBEES is reported but not trusted** (gap 117 bp, t = 9.8), because
  of its opening-price distortion (Phase 3).
- **Survivorship:** current constituents only. That flatters buy-and-hold
  more than the night/day split, but it is still only measured on survivors.
- CHECKPOINT: done. Subperiod table, stock counts with a multiple-testing
  bar, cross-section.

### Phase 5: Indian cost model
This is where the strategy lives or dies. It trades twice a day, about 250 round
trips a year, so a 0.25% round-trip cost is roughly 60% a year of drag.

Each component is a parameter in `config.py`. Check every rate against current
NSE, SEBI and broker schedules when implementing, because they change (F&O STT
changed in Oct 2024).

- **STT depends on trade type:**
  - Close -> open is a *delivery* trade (held overnight): about 0.1% on both buy
    and sell for stocks.
  - Open -> close is intraday: about 0.025%, sell side only.
  - ETF units: much lower, sell side (verify).
  - Futures: sell side only.
- **Other charges:**
  - stamp duty (buy side)
  - exchange transaction charge
  - SEBI fee
  - GST on brokerage and exchange charges
  - brokerage (zero for delivery at many discount brokers)
  - DP charge per stock per sell day. It's a flat amount, so it hits small
    accounts hardest, and brokers differ on whether BTST sells pay it.
- **Slippage:** half-spread at the open auction, plus the close mechanics
  (Phase 6).
- Output: a **break-even table** with, per instrument, the round-trip cost at
  which the night strategy's net return equals buy-and-hold, next to the
  estimated real cost.
- CHECKPOINT: break-even table.

### Phase 6: Tradable variants
- **S0** buy-and-hold (benchmark).
- **S1** long night: buy at close, sell at the next open. In India this is a
  BTST trade. Under T+1 you sell before the shares are delivered, which carries
  short-delivery/auction risk if the counterparty fails.
- **S2** short day: short at open, cover at close. Intraday cash shorting (MIS)
  is allowed, and STT is lower than for S1.
- **S3** S1 + S2 combined.
- **S4** execution-timing tilt, with no extra trades: a monthly SIP that buys at
  the close instead of the open. This measures what the pattern is worth to a
  long-term investor at zero added cost, and is probably the most practical
  takeaway.
- **S5** (optional) Nifty futures. Futures open/close data needs NSE's F&O
  bhavcopy because yfinance doesn't have it. Only worth doing if Phase 5 shows
  the ETF and stock versions die on costs and futures might not.

Execution realism:
- *Open:* NSE's opening price is set by the 09:00-09:08 pre-open call auction.
  An order can take part in it, so the open is achievable.
- *Close:* NSE's official close for stocks is the volume-weighted average of the
  last 30 minutes, not a single price you can trade at. A VWAP order over
  15:00-15:30 gets close to it. Model the leftover gap as slippage.
- CHECKPOINT: gross vs net equity curves, Sharpe ratio, max drawdown, turnover.
